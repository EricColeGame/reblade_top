## Why ReBlade Blade Upgrades Matter Right Now

The first commercial wind farms are being decommissioned, and their massive composite blades are notoriously hard to recycle. ReBlade blade upgrades offer a practical answer: instead of incinerating or burying end-of-life blades, the company transforms them into durable building panels. For asset owners, developers, and specifiers, these ReBlade blade upgrades turn a disposal liability into a usable, traceable product stream.

That matters because wind energy is only as clean as its end-of-life story. A turbine that generated two decades of low-carbon electricity shouldn't finish its life in a landfill. ReBlade's approach closes that loop by treating retired blades as a raw material feedstock rather than as waste — and by proving the model at a scale the industry can actually adopt.

This guide breaks down what the upgrades involve, how the material flows from wind farm to finished board, what the published specifications look like, and what to ask before you specify recycled blade content on a project.

## What Are ReBlade Blade Upgrades?

ReBlade is a circular-materials company focused on repurposing decommissioned wind turbine blades. Its flagship product, BladeBoard®, is a long-life panel made from recovered blade material and engineered for decking, fencing, and walkways. In this context, "upgrades" refers to the material transformation — taking a composite structure that has reached the end of its service life and re-engineering it into a higher-value product.

Each board contains a substantial share of recovered blade material, and the process is designed to squeeze maximum output from every tonne processed. The result is a product line that behaves like a conventional building panel while carrying an auditable circular origin story.

### Why the Material Efficiency Number Matters

Roughly 500 linear meters of finished product per tonne is the headline figure for anyone evaluating ReBlade blade upgrades commercially. It tells you how much usable product a decommissioning project can generate and gives procurement teams a way to model material yield before a single blade leaves the site.

## From Wind Farm to Fencing: How the Upgrade Process Works

The ReBlade blade upgrades process is built around traceability. Material is tracked from the moment a turbine comes down to the moment a finished board ships, which is what allows the company to make auditable circularity claims rather than vague sustainability statements.

| Step | What Happens | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Decommissioning | Retired blades are removed from site as part of wind farm repowering or teardown | Establishes the starting point of the material chain |
| 2. Segmented transport | Blades are cut down for safe, efficient logistics | Makes cross-country movement of oversized components feasible |
| 3. Material recovery | Blade composite is separated and prepared for processing | Determines the quality of the final feedstock |
| 4. Board forming | Recovered material is combined into panel form at the specified profile | Delivers consistent, specifiable product dimensions |
| 5. Finishing | Boards are cut, colored, and finished to order | Allows architectural and civic projects to match local design standards |
| 6. Traceability documentation | Each batch is logged through the chain of custody | Provides auditable proof of the circular journey |
| 7. Delivery and install | Finished panels go to decking, fencing, and walkway projects | Converts recovered material into community infrastructure |

### The Second Life Beyond Panels

Boards aren't the only output. ReBlade also produces an injection molding pellet derived from recovered blade material, aimed at higher-value manufacturing applications. That gives decommissioning projects more than one destination for recovered composite — a meaningful advantage when volumes are large and timelines are tight.

### The Cost Argument in Plain Terms

Blade disposal has historically been a line item with no upside: you pay to move it, you pay to process it, and you pay again if landfill taxes apply. ReBlade blade upgrades flip that equation by giving the material a buyer. Operators still pay for logistics and processing, but the material itself stops being pure cost and starts offsetting part of the decommissioning budget.

### The Reporting Argument

Renewable operators face growing pressure to demonstrate circularity in decommissioning plans and planning consultations. A documented, auditable material pathway is a far stronger position than a generic commitment to "explore recycling options." That's the real value of the traceability matrix — it turns an intention into evidence.

## What to Ask Before You Specify ReBlade Blade Upgrades

If you're evaluating recycled blade content for an upcoming project, the questions below will separate a genuine circular supply chain from a marketing claim.

- **What is the recovered content percentage?** Confirm the 45–50% blade material figure applies to the profile you're ordering.
- **What yield should we expect?** Ask how many linear meters per tonne your volume is projected to produce.
- **Can you supply the traceability matrix?** Request documentation covering the specific batch destined for your site.
- **Which profile suits the load case?** Work with the supplier on the 225×45 mm, 160×25 mm, or a bespoke specification.
- **How does end-of-life work?** Confirm the recyclability pathway for the installed product.
- **What color and finish options exist?** Establish color-to-specification lead times early.

## FAQ

**What exactly are ReBlade blade upgrades?**
They are the company's process for converting decommissioned wind turbine blades into new, usable products — primarily BladeBoard® panels for decking, fencing, and walkways, plus an injection molding pellet for manufacturing. The "upgrade" is a material upgrade: a retired composite structure becomes a specifiable building product.

**How much finished product comes from a tonne of blade material?**
ReBlade publishes a figure of roughly 500 linear meters of finished product per tonne of blade material processed, with each board containing 45–50% recovered blade material. That yield figure is the key input for modeling material output on a decommissioning project.

**Are the boards recyclable when their own service life ends?**
Yes. BladeBoard® is described as infinitely recyclable, which means the material can re-enter the production cycle rather than heading to landfill after its first installation. Combined with UV stability, that makes for a genuinely long-life, multi-cycle product.

**Can I order custom sizes and colors?**
Two standard profiles are offered — 225×45 mm and 160×25 mm — alongside bespoke sizing for projects with non-standard requirements. Boards can also be colored to specification, which makes ReBlade blade upgrades viable for design-led and civic work where appearance is part of the brief.
