### What to Look For When You Watch It Again

- **Impact frames.** Pause on the hits. Clean, readable impact frames usually mean the combat has real hit-stop behind it.
- **Enemy variety.** Count distinct enemy silhouettes. Fewer than four in a reveal trailer is worth noting.
- **Environment scale.** Wide shots hint at how large a single run space will be.
- **UI glimpses.** Any health bar, ability icon, or cooldown timer is a signal that systems exist under the cinematics.
- **The cut points.** Where the trailer cuts away is often where the developers are least ready to show.

None of this is a substitute for playing the demo, but it is a useful filter when a trailer is the only evidence available.

## How to Get the Most Out of the Demo

If you are coming to the game from the trailer alone, a little structure helps. The demo window is limited, so spend the first run learning rather than winning.

1. **Play the first run badly on purpose.** Push into fights you would normally avoid to learn enemy tells and dodge timing.
2. **Test one upgrade path at a time.** Stacking a single build type reveals how deep the synergy system goes.
3. **Watch the frame rate during heavy effects.** Cyberpunk visuals plus particle-heavy combat is a common performance pitfall.
4. **Note what the tutorial skips.** Anything unexplained is usually where the systems get interesting.
5. **Compare your experience to community reports.** If your impressions diverge sharply from the consensus, that is worth saying out loud in the Steam forums.

The gap between a good trailer and a good game is measured in exactly this kind of hands-on time. The ReBlade announcement trailer earned attention; the demo has to earn hours.
